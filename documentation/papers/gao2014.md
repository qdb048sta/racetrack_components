The six-state four-wheel prediction models omit wheel dynamics; this is an
explicit reduction, not the same state space as the ten-state simulation plant.
In the bicycle equations (2.13), the written tire forces are **per wheel**, hence
their factors of two. This implementation's bicycle uses axle-total forces and
therefore omits the extra factor of two; the four-wheel model uses per-wheel loads.

## Tire equations and source ambiguity

The printed pure-slip Pacejka expression is

\[
Y=D\sin\{C\arctan[Bx-E(Bx-\arctan(Bx))]\}+S_v,
\quad x=X+S_h.
\]

`magic_formula()` exposes it for inspection, but it is not the simulation tire.
The source does not supply the full combined-slip formulation or calibrated
parameter set, so this branch does not pretend to reproduce that model.

The runnable model uses the modified Fiala option. Set
`D=sqrt((mu*Fz)^2-Fx^2)` and `t=tan(alpha)`. Its implemented adhesion branch is

\[
F_y=-Ct+\frac{C^2}{3D}|t|t-\frac{C^3}{27D^2}t^3,
\quad |t|<3D/C,
\]

with `Fy=-D*sign(alpha)` beyond the threshold. At zero available lateral grip,
the force is zero. All slip laws use the wheel-velocity-minus-steer convention.

**Printed-equation correction:** Eq. 2.23 on PDF page 31 visibly prints `tan^2`
in its final term. The implementation uses `tan^3`: this restores odd symmetry
and gives the continuous value `-D` at `tan(alpha)=3D/C`, consistent with the
stated saturation law and Eq. 2.24. This is a disclosed correction, not a claim
that the PDF prints the implemented exponent. Tests check both properties.

## MPC algorithms extracted from the thesis

Chapter 3 compares a one-level NMPC (Eq. 3.12) with a point-mass high-level
replanner (3.14) and nonlinear low-level follower (3.13). Both penalize tracking
and input changes; the planner handles obstacles. Sec. 3.7 replaces the upper
planner with a clothoid construction for semi-autonomous intervention.

Chapter 4 replaces the point-mass planner with a spatial dynamic bicycle. For
road curvature `kappa`, lateral offset `ey` and heading error `epsi`,

\[
\dot s=\frac{v_x\cos e_\psi-v_y\sin e_\psi}{1-\kappa e_y},
\quad x'=\dot x/\dot s,\quad
e_\psi'=r/\dot s-\kappa,\quad
e_y'=\frac{v_x\sin e_\psi+v_y\cos e_\psi}{\dot s},\quad t'=1/\dot s.
\]

This change of independent variable makes obstacle positions known at the
optimization nodes. Eqs. 4.7-4.9 use state/input/rate bounds, tracking penalties
and input blocking. A passing side is chosen first, then obstacles become
station-dependent bounds on `ey`. The result is converted back to world
coordinates and interpolated in time. The lower controller uses a linearization
of the four-wheel dynamics and the tracking cost (4.10), with steering and
differential braking. The thesis reports NPSOL at high level and SQP/QP methods
at low level (printed p. 54). Its example high-level grid is 1.5 m with 15 nodes;
the low-level interval is 0.05 s with a five-step horizon (p. 55).

Chapter 5 adds robustness: reduced nominal dynamics, an offline invariant error
set, tightened road/obstacle/slip constraints, and an ancillary correction
`u=ubar+K*e`. Eq. 5.37 (p. 74 / PDF 87) is the nominal finite-horizon program,
including soft constraints and input blocking. Its robustness conclusion is
conditional on the admissible uncertainty bounds and nominal feasibility.

## Implemented adaptation

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `FourWheel` in `tracklab/paper_models/gao2014.py` | Six chassis states `[X,Y,psi,vx,vy,r]`; independent left/right ideal longitudinal effort; static wheel loads and Fiala lateral forces |
| `SpatialBicycle` | `[vy,vx,r,epsi,ey,t]` and `[steer,effort]`; spatial chain rule above |
| `SpatialMPC` | RK4 in station, hard width/forward-progress constraints, 3-node input blocking, logged soft obstacle corridor |
| `TemporalMPC` | Nonlinear time-domain multiple shooting using the four-wheel chassis; interpolated state and input references |
| `run_paper_gao.py` | Creates the real CSV/interpolated road, both controllers and the simulation plant |

The high-level steering increments are conservatively limited for a maximum
speed of 30 m/s and the actual previous low-level command is fed back to the
planner. The temporal tracker also penalizes deviation from planned inputs.
These two choices are integration adaptations. The temporal controller here is
nonlinear and solved by IPOPT, not the thesis's specific LTV/SQP implementation.
Both sides can drive or brake; effort is converted directly to `mu*Fz` fractions.
There is no calibrated wheel-torque/brake allocator. The body plant is integrated
with RK4 substeps of at most 5 ms; it is not the ten-state experimental simulator.

The road coordinate singularities are exposed: `1-kappa*ey>=0.2` and positive
forward projected velocity are required. Friction is a constant model parameter,
as in the source's assumptions, rather than an online estimate or terrain map.

## Run and limits

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements-driving.txt
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python run_paper_gao.py --steps 40
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_paper_gao.py' -v
```

The optional `--obstacle` introduces a preselected left-passing corridor.
The demonstrated required center offset is 1 m over stations 25-35 m. The
planner targets a 1.4 m lower bound, adding a 0.4 m heuristic tracking margin.
Obstacle bounds have nonnegative slack with a large linear/quadratic penalty;
road widths remain hard. This is a disclosed adaptation inspired by the soft
constraint idea in Eq. 5.37, without its robust invariant-set construction.
Both maximum planner slack and sampled physical corridor violation are logged.
The runner does not interpret a successful soft-constrained solve as proof of
clearance. The tested 100-step run logs zero sampled physical violation, with
about 0.133 m maximum planned-margin slack. No continuous-time footprint or
perception uncertainty guarantee is inferred from that result.
It is a deterministic demonstration, not an obstacle perception/side-selection
system or robust collision-avoidance certificate. Solver failures are raised,
not converted silently into successful runs. See `VALIDATION_gao2014.md` for
the exact tested commands and any remaining scenario limitation.

Seven unit tests cover force symmetry/capacity, numerical-symbolic agreement,
force/yaw signs, spatial chain rule, independent time integration and both
optimization layers. The 31 baseline road/Python-driving tests pass.

Not implemented: calibrated Pacejka combined slip, wheel-spin simulation,
clothoid intervention logic, Chapter 5 robust sets/gain, experimental timing or
full-lap racing optimization. The new modules do not change the native OCD tire
selection or its eleven-state predictor.
